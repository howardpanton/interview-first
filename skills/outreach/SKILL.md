# Outreach

Load the `job-search-core` skill and read the candidate profile first.

## Why this matters

A cold application to a popular role competes with hundreds of others and is often ranked
by software before a person sees it. A short message to the right person gets read by a
person. For strong-fit roles, reach a person first, or at the same time.

## Who to contact, best first

1. **Someone you already know** at the company, or a former colleague who knows someone.
2. **The hiring manager** (often the person the role reports to; check the listing and
   LinkedIn).
3. **The person who posted the role,** or the recruiter named on it.
4. **An engineer or team member in the same team,** to ask what it's like. Not to ask for
   a referral in the first message.

## Message types

Write each message so it can be read in 20 seconds on a phone.

| Type | Length | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Connection note | Under 300 characters | Who you are in one line, why them, one easy ask |
| Hiring manager | 60 to 120 words | The role, your single strongest piece of evidence for it, a soft ask (a short call, or "I've applied and wanted to put a name to it") |
| Referral request (someone you know) | 60 to 120 words | Warm opener, the role, why you fit in one line, ask if they'd be comfortable referring you, make saying no easy |
| Learn about the team (someone you don't know) | 50 to 90 words | What drew you to the team, one specific question, no ask for a referral |
| Recruiter reply | 50 to 100 words | Interest, availability, ask for the salary band and the process before giving your own number |
| Follow-up | 40 to 70 words | Seven working days after applying or after a call; one new piece of value or a simple check-in, never guilt |
| Thank-you after interview | 60 to 100 words | Same day, one thing you enjoyed discussing, one point you'd add to an answer |

## Rules

- If it's unclear who the message is for or what the ask is, ask one question first.
- One message, one ask.
- Only use contact details the person has made public for work (a LinkedIn profile, a work
  email on the listing). Don't guess or look up personal email addresses or phone numbers.
- Don't share the user's CV or personal details with anyone the user hasn't chosen.
- Specific beats flattering: mention something real about their product, post or team.
- Never pretend to know someone, never imply a referral that hasn't been given.
- Don't attach the CV to a first message unless asked; a link to a portfolio or profile
  is lighter.
- On salary: ask for their band first. If pressed, give a range based on the user's
  target in the profile, not their current pay.
- Give 2 or 3 variants when the relationship is unclear (for example "warm", "light
  touch", "direct").

## Sending

You draft; the user sends. Only send a message on their behalf if a tool allows it and
they explicitly ask for that specific message to be sent. Offer to log each message in
the tracker with a follow-up date (the `application-tracker` skill), and to set a
reminder for the follow-up if scheduling is available.
