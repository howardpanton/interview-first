# How screeners read your CV

Many employers use an applicant tracking system (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, iCIMS
and others). Increasingly these rank or summarise applicants with AI before a recruiter
looks. Exactly how each one works varies by employer and isn't public, so treat this as a
practical model, not a specification.

## What tends to matter

1. **Knock-outs.** Form questions about location, right to work, office days, years of
   experience in a named area, degrees or clearances. A "wrong" answer can remove you
   before anything else is read. Answer honestly; if one rules you out, contact a person
   instead.
2. **The core skill.** Every role is really about one or two things. If your CV shows
   them weakly, a high overall keyword match often isn't enough.
3. **Must-have terms.** The listing's requirements, in its own words or close synonyms.
4. **Title family.** Whether one of your job titles looks like the one being hired for.
5. **Readability.** Whether the system can pull your text out cleanly.

This is what the screener score in `job-fit-score` estimates.

## A pattern worth knowing

Fast rejections (two to four days) on cold applications usually mean a screen did the
work. When several arrive together, look at what they share: the route, the screener
score and the role's core skill. In practice it's often the core skill, even when overall
keyword coverage looked high.

## Make your CV easy to read

- One column. No text boxes, no tables for layout, no skill bars or icons in place of
  words.
- Standard headings: Summary, Experience, Skills, Education.
- Contact details in the body, not in the page header or footer.
- Each role: title, company and dates on clear lines.
- Save as a text-based PDF (not a scan or image). Check by selecting the text in a PDF
  viewer.
- Use the listing's words for things you've genuinely done ("design system" if they say
  design system, not only "component library").

## What not to do

- Don't paste the listing in white text, or stuff keywords into a hidden section. Systems
  and recruiters catch it, and it ends the application.
- Don't add skills you don't have. Getting through the screen only moves the problem to
  the interview.

## When the screener is against you

If the role is a strong match for you but your CV can't honestly pass the screen (a core
skill you have but only thinly, or an unusual background), go through a person: a
referral, the hiring manager or the recruiter. A person can make a judgement a filter
can't.
