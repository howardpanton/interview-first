# The "true but missing" exercise

Most experienced people leave a third of their best evidence off their CV. It felt ordinary
at the time, it was "just part of the job", or it happened years ago. Screeners can only
match what's written down, and interviewers can only ask about what they can see.

This exercise finds those things. It takes about 45 minutes. Do it with Claude: paste your
CV and ask it to run this exercise with you, one section at a time.

## 1. Ask the questions

For each role in the last ten years, answer quickly. A rough note is enough.

- **People:** Did you hire, interview, onboard, mentor, run 1:1s, set objectives, do
  appraisals, manage a budget, cover for a manager?
- **Scope:** Did you act up into a bigger role, even for a few months? Lead a project
  across teams? Own something nobody else understood?
- **Decisions:** What technical, design or process decisions did you make that are still
  in use? What did you choose not to do?
- **Numbers:** Team size, users, customers, releases, speed, cost, time saved, errors
  reduced, revenue touched. Rough is fine; you can say "about".
- **Working with others:** Product, design, sales, customers, support, leadership. Did you
  talk to users, run research, present to senior people?
- **Quality and delivery:** Tests, pipelines, monitoring, incidents you handled, standards
  you set.
- **New things:** Tools or practices you brought in (AI tools included), and whether
  others adopted them.
- **Outside work:** Side projects, writing, talks, open source, courses, certificates.

## 2. Sort the answers

Put each answer into one of four groups, matching your profile:

- **Strong:** you could give an example in an interview tomorrow.
- **Limited:** real, but thin. Write down exactly how thin ("one production site in
  2019").
- **Unknown:** you're not sure it counts. Ask someone who worked with you.
- **Gap:** you haven't done it. Write it down anyway, so nothing ever claims it.

## 3. Compare with your CV

For every Strong and Limited item, check whether your CV says it, in the words a job
listing would use. Mark each one:

- **On the CV**
- **True but missing:** add to your profile now; add to the CV where it fits or keep it
  for tailoring.
- **Too small to matter:** leave it in the profile only.

## 4. Watch for these common misses

- People management done without the title ("hiring", "1:1s", "objectives").
- "Cross-functional", "stakeholders", "end to end": phrases many listings use for work
  you probably do daily.
- Acting or covering roles.
- Design, research or user-testing work done by engineers, and engineering work done by
  designers.
- Measurement: if you tracked adoption, cost or quality, say so.
- Old but relevant domains (an industry you left years ago may be exactly what a listing
  wants).

## The rule

Only true things go on a CV. This exercise is about finding what's true and missing, not
about stretching what's there.
