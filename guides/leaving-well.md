# Leaving well: redundancy, notice and starting again

Practical points for people whose role is ending, written for any country. Laws and
entitlements differ a lot between countries and contracts, so check your own position with
your country's official guidance, a union, or an employment lawyer. This isn't legal or
financial advice.

## In the first week

- **Get the details in writing:** the reason, the timeline, any consultation process, your
  notice period and what you'll be paid.
- **Read your contract** for notice, pay in lieu of notice, garden leave, holiday, bonus,
  share options, restrictive covenants and benefits.
- **Look up your country's rules** on redundancy pay, consultation, notice and
  unemployment support. Official government sites are the most reliable start.
- **Keep copies** of anything you're entitled to keep: contract, payslips, appraisals,
  references, records of roles you acted up into. Don't take confidential company
  material.
- **Tell the people who matter to you.** It is easier with support.

## Questions worth asking your employer

- When is my last day, and can it move if I find a role sooner?
- Will notice be worked, paid in lieu or spent on garden leave? (Pay in lieu usually lets
  you start a new job straight away; garden leave usually doesn't.)
- What happens to pension contributions, health cover, bonus and share options?
- Is there an enhanced package, and does it need a settlement agreement?
- Will I get a reference, and what will it say?
- Can I have time off to look for work and attend interviews?

## Telling new employers

- Be brief, honest and neutral: "My role is being made redundant in a restructure, so I'm
  available from [date]." Redundancy is about the role, not you, and interviewers know it.
- Don't criticise your employer, even if it was handled badly.
- Don't promise a start date until your leaving terms are confirmed.
- If you can start quickly, say so. It can be a real advantage.

## Money

- Work out your monthly essentials and how long your savings and any payout last. Knowing
  the number makes decisions calmer.
- Check whether any payout is taxed and how, in your country.
- If you're tempted to accept the first offer out of worry, compare it with your
  constraints in the profile first.

## Looking after yourself

Rejections in a job search are mostly about screens, timing and numbers. Keep a routine,
keep moving, see people, and set a time each day when you stop. If it's getting on top of
you, talk to someone you trust or a professional.
