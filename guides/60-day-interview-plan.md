# The 60-day interview plan

A plan aimed at **conversations with people who can hire you**, not at application counts.

The numbers below are working targets for an experienced professional looking full time.
They are not market statistics. Adjust them to your hours, your field and your market, and
change them when your own numbers tell you to.

## The idea in one paragraph

Popular roles get hundreds of applicants in the first few days, and software ranks most of
them before a person reads anything. Sending more cold applications mostly adds to that
pile. What moves you forward is fewer, better-aimed applications, each paired with a
message to a real person, and a CV that says the true things screeners look for. Measure
conversations, not submissions.

## Before day 1 (one or two sessions)

- [ ] Fill in your profile (`/interview-first:setup`). Be honest about Strong, Limited and Gap.
- [ ] Do the "true but missing" exercise in `cv-gaps-exercise.md`.
- [ ] Build your master CV, then 2 or 3 base versions, one per lane.
- [ ] Update your LinkedIn or equivalent profile to match the lead version: headline,
      about section, the same job titles and dates as your CV.
- [ ] List 25 target companies you'd genuinely like to work for, and who you know at each.
- [ ] Set up the tracker and, if you can, a daily search.

## Weekly activity targets

| Activity | Target per week | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Applications | 5 to 8 | Match score 65 or more, strongest first. More than 8 usually means less time for people |
| Outreach messages | 20 to 25 | About 5 a weekday: tied to applications, former colleagues, target companies, follow-ups |
| Conversations | 3 | Any real exchange: a recruiter call, a coffee, a call with someone in the team |
| Public posts or comments | 2 posts or 15 comments | Optional, but it raises profile views from recruiters |
| Skill-gap work | Your choice | Publish what you build the week you finish it, so it counts as evidence |
| Interview practice | 2 hours | One mock interview and one timed practical exercise |
| Review | 30 minutes, once a week | `/interview-first:review` |

## Outcome targets

Aims to steer by, not forecasts. Lead-level hiring often takes four to eight weeks from
first call to offer, and holiday periods slow everything down.

| By | First conversations (recruiter screens count) | Later-stage interviews | Other |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Day 14 | 2 | 0 | CV versions live; 25 target companies listed |
| Day 30 | 5 | 2 | About 25 applications; first read on which CV version works |
| Day 45 | 8 | 3 | |
| Day 60 | 10 to 12 | 4 | At least one process at final stage |

## If the numbers fall short

| Signal | What to change |
| --- | --- |
| Fewer than 2 first conversations by day 21 | Check the route first: were the applications cold? Move to contact-first for every strong role |
| Fast rejections (2 to 4 days) | That's a screen. Check the screener scores and the core skill of each role; tailor harder or go through a person |
| A CV version has 8 applications and no conversations | Rewrite that version's title line and summary only, then test again |
| Outreach reply rate under 1 in 10 after 30 messages | Rewrite the opening line; try a different kind of contact |
| Fewer than 3 first conversations by day 30 | Widen: an adjacent lane, contract roles, more locations or one level down |
| First calls but no second interviews | The problem has moved to interviews. Double the mock practice and review the questions you were asked |

Change one thing at a time, so you can tell what worked.

## A sample weekday (about 2 to 3 hours)

| When | What | How long |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start of day | Read the daily search results. Decide on each role: apply, skip or watch | 10 minutes |
| | `/interview-first:today`: follow-ups and next actions due | 10 minutes |
| | Outreach: about five messages | 40 minutes |
| Middle | One tailored application (score, tailor CV, letter, message a person) | 60 to 90 minutes |
| End of day | Update the tracker; prepare tomorrow's first message | 10 minutes |

Keep the rest of the day for learning, practice, exercise and people. A job search run at
full stretch for 60 days burns out; one run steadily doesn't.
